Node Graph

Your workspace drawn the way Rust nests it. Pick a symbol and see everything a change to it would reach.

  • Shockwave rings

    Select a symbol and its dependents light up on concentric rings, hot at the epicenter and cooling with each hop. Lines trace the path the blast travels.

  • Dependents or dependencies

    Flip between what breaks if this changes and what this symbol relies on.

    D

  • Adjustable reach

    Widen or narrow the blast one hop at a time, from direct dependents out to twelve hops.

    []

  • Connections pane right sidebar

    Everything the blast reaches, in hop order with ring-colored badges, counted in symbols and in files. Click a row to fly there while the selection stays pinned at the top.

    C

  • Nested circles

    Packages hold modules, modules hold types, and types hold their own fields, variants and methods. Impl blocks fold into the type they implement.

  • Spring and gravity arrangements

    Spring solves a tidy layout that keeps every symbol in its module. Gravity lets references pull, so code organized one way and used another shows the difference.

    Shift+A

  • Three blast shapes

    Off leaves the map still. Pull tows dependents toward the selection without leaving their modules. Radial seats modules on rings by hop.

    Shift+P

  • Context stays on screen

    Code outside the blast dims, so the shape of the workspace stays behind the answer. Or hide it and let what's left pack closed.

  • Every symbol, at every zoom

    Nothing collapses into a summary and no symbol covers another. Each keeps its dot at every zoom; only the names fade in as you get closer.

  • Group by modules or files

    Switch between the module tree and the folder tree instantly, with no re-index. Crates that share a folder pack side by side.

    G

  • Fast at scale

    Gravity uses the fast multipole method: 16 ms per step for 16,000 bodies, where a direct sum takes 310 ms. Both arrangements solve off the main thread.

  • Colored by kind

    Functions, methods, structs, fields, enums, variants, traits, trait methods, type aliases, consts, statics, macros and modules each have their own color and icon.

  • Names that stay readable

    Labels carry their kind icon and slide apart instead of overlapping. Name every symbol, or only the ones you're looking at.

    Shift+L

  • Freeze

    Hold either arrangement still with one switch. Dragging still works while frozen.

    Shift+F

  • Drag anything

    Drag a dot to move one symbol, or a circle's rim to move its whole subtree. The layout keeps settling around what you hold.

  • View a container in isolation

    Open any circle's menu to show it on its own. One click on the toolbar brings the rest back.

  • Hidden pane right sidebar

    Hide any circle from its menu. Hidden circles are listed here with their paths, one click from coming back.

    N

  • Animated filtering

    Filtered symbols swell and shrink away, so you can see what left and why the layout moved.

Thermal Vision Profiler

Run your program under a sampling profiler and color the map by where the time goes. Windows 8 or later.

  • Heat on the graph

    The thermal view colors each function from cold to hot by inclusive time, and thickens call edges by the samples that crossed them.

    Shift+V

  • Profile in one click

    Profile runs add debug info to release builds without touching Cargo.toml, then sample the call stack 8,000 times a second through ETW. Windows asks for administrator access once per run.

  • Profile pane right sidebar

    The hottest functions ranked by share of the run. Pick one to see its self and inclusive time and its top callers and callees, and to fly the camera to it.

    P

  • Saved profiles

    Every capture is saved to the profile pane. Recall, rename or delete it later. Profiles survive re-indexing.

  • Run from the toolbar

    Build and run any binary in release or debug, through cargo run or the executable directly.

Flow Graph

Read what a function calls as an outline of cards.

  • Call outline

    A function's callees as indented cards, one level per step. It opens on the selected function, or on your program's main.

  • Jump along an arrow

    Hover an arrow and a control rides along it, naming the card at the far end. Click to fly there.

  • Unfold as you go

    A new flow shows the function and its direct callees. Unfold one level at a time, or everything at once.

    XZ

  • Signatures with type chips

    Each card shows its owner and signature. Hover a type for its full path; click it to select it.

  • Open the call site

    Right-click an arrow to open each of its call sites in your editor.

  • Wires that stay clear

    Arrows run in their own colored lanes, and cards move down so no wire crosses one. Recursion shows as a dimmer back edge.

  • Re-root anywhere

    Choose Show in Flow Graph from any function's menu, or click a function chip to make it the subject.

  • Export to SVG

    Write the current flow to flow.svg with one key.

    E

Code Health

Find the code nothing uses, and see what your changes reach.

  • Blast your working tree

    Seed a blast from your uncommitted changes. Staged, unstaged and untracked edits all count, matched to the symbols whose bodies they touch.

    Alt+G

  • Dead code

    Walks out from main, tests and trait impls and flags everything never reached, including chains of dead code that only call each other.

  • Unreferenced symbols

    The browser's unreferenced tab lists workspace symbols nothing references, and says how many of them are tests, benches or examples.

    2

  • Working-tree diff

    The browser's diff tab lists the symbols your uncommitted changes touch, refreshed each time you open it.

    3

  • Reports without the window

    --diff prints your working tree's blast radius, --stats counts kinds and the most-referenced symbols, and --svg renders the layout. Run them from a script or CI.

  • Filters for every view

    Show only unreferenced code or only dead code, in the graph and the lists. The filter is remembered between sessions.

  • Tests kept out of the way

    Tests, benches and examples are read from source (#[test], #[tokio::test], #[cfg(test)], tests/, benches/, examples/) and hidden until you want them.

    Shift+TShift+BShift+E

  • Notices log

    Indexing failures, config problems and every warning collect behind the bell at the foot of the right rail, with repeats folded and a copy button on each message.

Refactoring

Structural edits from the graph. Each one shows you its plan before a file is written.

  • Rename across the workspace

    Rename a symbol everywhere it's used, including trait methods with their impls, field shorthand, intra-doc links, and a module's file or folder. Every keystroke is checked for clashes.

  • Move a module

    Choose Move, click the destination circle and read the plan. Paths keep their style, use groups split where needed, and visibility widens only as far as it must.

  • Plan first, then write

    Every check runs before the first write. Writes are atomic, rustfmt only touches files that were already formatted, and the graph re-indexes when the edit lands.

  • Promote to a crate

    Turn a module into its own crate, with a manifest that inherits from the workspace and entries in members and [workspace.dependencies].

  • Demote to a module

    Fold a library crate into another as a module. Its bins, tests, examples and benches come along, its dependencies and features merge in, and you decide what happens to its other folders.

  • Knows when to refuse

    Moves that would create a dependency cycle or break the orphan rule are refused with the reason, and so is any file that changed since the last index.

  • Dissolve a module

    Move a module's contents into its parent and remove the empty module.

  • Rename folders

    Rename a folder and the manifest paths, include! paths and #[path] attributes that point into it follow.

  • Create from the graph

    Start new projects (a package or a workspace, with git), crates, modules in either file layout, and plain files, each declared where it belongs.

Keyboard First

Every command has a key, and the camera follows wherever you go.

  • One axis in and out

    Right always goes one level further in. Left always backs out. Up and down move within a level.

    →←↑↓

  • Browser left sidebar

    A tree of packages, modules, types and symbols, with tabs for unreferenced code, your working-tree diff and your projects. Chains of single-child folders fold into one row.

    Tab1234

  • The camera follows

    Walking the tree flies the camera to each row, framing a container at its circle's scale and a symbol at its own.

  • Fuzzy search

    Type a few letters of any symbol. Word starts and consecutive letters rank first.

    /

  • History pane right sidebar

    Step back and forward through the places you looked, with the view, direction and hop count restored. The history pane shows the whole trail.

    Alt+←Alt+→H

  • Open in your editor

    Jump to the definition in VS Code, VSCodium, Cursor, Zed, Sublime Text, Helix, Neovim, Vim, IntelliJ IDEA, RustRover or Emacs, or any command you configure.

    O

  • Tour a blast

    With a symbol selected, the arrows step through its connections and the camera flies to each one. Right makes the highlighted symbol the new selection.

  • Rebind every key

    Bind any command to a key or a Ctrl, Alt or Shift chord. Tooltips show the live binding, and a key that's taken is refused with its current command named.

    Ctrl+,

  • Frame anything

    Fit the blast or the whole graph. Double-click a circle to frame it, or empty canvas to see everything.

    F

  • Switch views

    Move between the Node Graph and the Flow Graph. Your selection comes with you.

    V

  • A key for every pane

    The browser, connections, symbols, history, hidden and profile panes each open with one key. Shift+Tab hides the right sidebar or brings back its last pane.

    TabCSHNPShift+Tab

  • Escape unwinds one layer

    Escape closes the topmost thing first: a popup, then a pane, then settings, then the selection.

    Esc

Customization

Make it look and behave the way you work.

  • 209 themes

    Every Helix theme with colors of its own, plus built-in dark and light. Filter by dark, light, vivid, pastel, muted, warm, cool or mono, or search by name.

  • Symbols pane right sidebar

    Every symbol kind in your workspace with its count, a switch and a color. Recolor a kind everywhere it appears, switch it off and let the layout pack closed, or Shift+click to show it alone.

    S

  • Edit and save themes

    Adjust any of 16 colors with an HSL picker or a hex value, and save the result as your own theme.

  • Fonts and text size

    Use any installed font, size each of nine text roles, and step all text larger or smaller from the keyboard.

    Ctrl+=Ctrl+-Ctrl+0

  • Smooth on high-refresh displays

    Vsync is off by default, so a 240 Hz display isn't held to 60 frames a second. When nothing is changing, the app sleeps.

  • A config file you can edit

    Settings save to config.toml as you change them. Your comments and key order are kept, and new keys arrive with a description.

  • Background image

    Put an image behind the graph, with its own opacity and blend mode.

  • Resizable sidebars

    Drag either sidebar to the width you want. It's remembered.

  • Searchable settings

    One search box across every settings tab, matching names and descriptions.
